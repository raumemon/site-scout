Site Scout Spokane / Coeur d'Alene

What this is: a restaurant site-selection tool, currently tuned for a gyro / Mediterranean fast-casual concept. It divides the Spokane–CdA corridor into ~15,000 hexagons, each about the size of a city block (~400 ft across), and scores every one on where a new restaurant would do best. It fuses 18 data systems — federal (Census, Bureau of Labor Statistics), state (WSDOT, Idaho ITD, WA Liquor Board), city (Spokane Valley traffic), county (assessor sale records, permits), and live commercial and social feeds (Google, Crexi, STA transit, Reddit, six local news outlets) — including 2,277 food businesses carrying 1.2 million customer reviews, 1,494 commercial property sales, and 9,714 traffic counts.

The comparable tools are what national chains use for the same decision: Placer.ai (foot-traffic analytics, reported ~$15K–$50K/yr), Buxton (site scoring, reported ~$25K–$75K/yr), SiteZeus (reported ~$25K+/yr), Esri Business Analyst (~$4K+/yr), plus CoStar for the real-estate side (~$6K–$12K/yr per seat). This tool rebuilds most of that stack from public sources, and adds local intelligence (Reddit, local news) that none of them carry. What it lacks vs. Placer: phone-GPS foot traffic counts.

What's under the hood: who lives and works within walking range (Census), each area's yearly dining spend in dollars, every competitor with ratings and review counts (Google), traffic counts, bus service, parking, liquor licenses and brand-new openings, sold commercial land prices, active lease listings, and what locals actually say on Reddit about each neighborhood ("Street talk").

How to use it:

  • Opportunity — overall best places to open. Value — best score per dollar of property cost. Sentiment — combined signal from Reddit street talk, local news tone, and review quality.
  • Delivery play — a gravity model of who you'd actually capture. Each zone's yearly dining budget is computed from its households (Census counts × federal spending-by-income data) plus lunch money from people who work there. Those dollars are then split between every existing restaurant and a hypothetical new entrant, where a venue's share depends on how strong it is (review volume) and how far away it is in real drive minutes (16-minute cap, so rivers and freeway crossings cost what they actually cost). The new entrant's summed winnings across all zones = the "Est. new-site capture $/yr" you see in popups.
  • Residents / Workers / Lunch / Dinner — who's around, and when.
  • Recommended tab — pockets with lots of people and no real food nearby.
  • Pin an address — paste any address (e.g. from a listing) to get its full site report; pin two or more and hit Compare.
  • Adjust weighting — the sliders re-score the whole map live.

Dollar figures are estimates for comparing sites against each other, not revenue projections. Click any hex for its full report. Data pulled September 2026. Spot something wrong about a place you know? Report it — local knowledge makes the model better.
